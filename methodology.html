How the worksheet scores a system

Version 2 of the method. Capacity and selfhood are scored separately: SIU estimates how much of an architecture a system has, and three self tracks describe what kind of self, if any, runs on it.

Status: a proposal. The reference scores below were all made by one surveyor and serve as a development set. The reliability and validation work that would turn the bands into results is described at the end and has not been done. The calculator runs everything on this page. The v1 rubric is kept as the legacy methodology.

What changed from v1

Version 1 multiplied three slider estimates, divided by a human baseline, and called anything above 50% of human self-aware. That design had three problems. A product of small nonzero numbers never reaches zero, so a thermostat or a planet always scored something. A single self-aware cutoff mislabeled whole groups of animals: a dog reads human gaze and intent with great skill and fails the visual mirror test, and one number cannot hold both facts. And the sliders were estimates with no fixed items behind them, so two people could not be expected to reach the same score.

Version 2 keeps the three terms and changes everything around them:

  • Each term is scored from five fixed items, so the score can be checked item by item.
  • The terms are combined with a geometric mean, and a gate on the weakest term does the conjunctive work.
  • Unity has an entry rung, a mapped sensory hierarchy. A system without one scores a true zero.
  • Scores fall into coarse bands set by frozen reference species, and percent of human is gone.
  • Self-awareness is scored on its own, as three tracks, instead of as the top slice of SIU.
  • A short tier check decides whether a system is scored at all.

The species database still holds v1 scores and has not been rescored on this worksheet.

What the band measures

The Integration items follow Ginsburg and Jablonka's sequence of learning, which they tie to minimal consciousness. The Unity items follow Feinberg and Mallatt's account of mapped sensory hierarchies, which they tie to sensory consciousness. The band is therefore an estimate of how much of that architecture a system has. It inherits those two accounts' inference from marker to experience and adds nothing to it. Anyone who rejects either account should read the band as a graded profile of cognitive architecture and nothing more.

One consequence is stated openly: an individual ant clears the gate, because it learns in an open-ended way on a mapped sensory hierarchy. That is a claim about its architecture, not about what, if anything, it is like to be an ant.

Step 0: The tiers

Before scoring capacity, place the system. This step has no points.

  • Tier 1, persistence. Holds and rebuilds its own form while energy flows through it, with no ratchet. Flames, hurricanes, stars, the Earth. The word life is kept for Tier 2.
  • Tier 2, Darwinian life. Builds and repairs its own parts inside its own boundary, and belongs to a reproducing, selectable lineage. Trees, animals, bacteria.
  • Tier 3, awareness. A Tier 2 system that clears the SIU gate. Its self tracks are then scored.
  • Constructs. Made rather than evolved. Placed beside the ladder and scored on SIU like anything else. An aware construct that does not reproduce would be a kind of virtual mule: aware, sterile, made by members of a lineage without being one.

The questions, in order:

  1. Made or evolved? Made means construct; go to Step 1.
  2. Does it keep its form only while energy or matter flows through it? No means it is not on the ladder (a rock, a crystal, a corpse).
  3. When disturbed, does it restore its own form? No means a passing pattern. Yes means Tier 1.
  4. Does it build and repair its own parts inside its own boundary? No means Tier 1, and SIU is not scored.
  5. Did it come from parents like itself, through heredity with variation? Yes means Tier 2. Sterile members of a reproducing lineage count (worker ants, mules). A virus has a lineage without self-production; record it as borderline Tier 2 and expect it to fail the gate.

Capacity: SIU, the gate and the bands

A = (S × I × U)1/3

Each term runs from 0 to 1 and is the sum of five items. A term at zero gives zero, which is the necessity claim: sensing with nothing integrating it is a camera, and integration with no unified perspective is a disconnected network. The cube root keeps the product's order and its collapse to zero, and it puts the result back on the 0 to 1 scale of the terms.

The cube root is still a mean, so above zero a weak term can be partly offset by strong ones. Sensing at 0.2 with the other two at 1 gives A of 0.58. The Human Development Index made the same move to a geometric mean in 2010, and the standard criticism of it is that it hides tradeoff rates that are hard to defend (Ravallion 2012). So the worksheet does not rely on the formula for conjunction. It reports min(S, I, U) beside A, and the gate requires it.

The gate. A system is scored for a self only if all three hold:

  • A is at least 0.35;
  • min(S, I, U) is at least 0.35;
  • open-ended learning (I2) is at least Partial.

A system that fails the gate is recorded as band None, with B, IP and NC as None.

How the cutoffs were set. Five reference species were placed in bands before any other case was scored, then scored on the worksheet, and each cutoff was fixed at the midpoint between neighboring anchors, rounded to 0.05. The cutoffs are frozen: they change only by re-running the anchors, never to move a particular case.

Read the bands for what they are. Sensing and Unity sit near 1 for nearly every animal with a centralized brain, so for those animals A is close to the cube root of Integration, and the cutoffs work out to Integration thresholds of about 0.04, 0.51 and 0.86. For vertebrates the bands are Integration bands sitting on a floor. The three-term structure does its real work elsewhere: Unity spreads for cephalopods, and both Sensing and Unity spread for constructs and distributed systems.

The floor. Unity's entry rung asks for a mapped sensory hierarchy. A system without one, such as a thermostat, an electron or the nematode C. elegans, scores U of 0 and so A of 0. That zero is a rule of the instrument, defended from Feinberg and Mallatt rather than derived. Three mapped levels is a convention, set to match the smallest brains that also show the behavioral items, so the jump from C. elegans at 0 to the fruit fly at 1.0 on Unity belongs to the rubric, not to nature. The floor does not refute panpsychism, which is a claim about experience. It only records that below the entry rung the instrument finds no structure to score.

Step 1: The items

Score each item Yes (0.2), Partial (0.1) or No (0). Sensing items are scored on their own. Integration and Unity are ladders ordered by difficulty: an item counts only if the one before it scored at least Partial, and the ladder stops at the first No. I5 sits outside the ladder and is always scored for organisms.

Unknown is not No. Mark it U, score the term twice, once with every U as No and once as Yes, and report the range. On the No branch the ladder stops at the U item; on the Yes branch it continues. Tag each answer with its evidence: F for field observation, L for lab experiment, N for neuroanatomy, R for inference from a related species (lowest confidence).

Sensing (S)

S1Dedicated sensors. Yes: receptor cells gathered into sense organs, such as eyes, antennae or a lateral line. Partial: specialized cells not gathered into organs. No: only general cell responses.
S2Number of senses. Yes: four or more distinct modalities. Partial: two or three.
S3Distance sense. Detects things it is not touching, with direction. Yes: vision, hearing, smell tracked to a source, electroreception, echolocation. Partial: light versus dark only, or a diffuse gradient.
S4Body sense. Senses its own posture or limb position, and an internal state such as hunger. Yes: both. Partial: one.
S5Fine discrimination. Yes: tells apart individuals of its own species, or differences as fine in some sense. Partial: coarse categories only.

Integration (I)

I1Associative learning. Links a cue to an outcome by classical or operant conditioning. Partial: habituation or sensitization only.
I2Open-ended learning. Learns new compound stimuli or new action sequences, including second-order or trace conditioning and cues combined across senses (unlimited associative learning). Partial: one sense or a few cue types.
I3Flexible problem-solving. Solves a problem it has not met before without step-by-step training, such as a detour around a new barrier or a new use of a tool. Partial: only after long trial and error, or only some individuals. Planning results go under N3 instead, so one study cannot raise the band and then be counted again under the cap that band sets.
I4Rule transfer. Applies a learned rule (same versus different, a number, a sequence) to stimuli it has never seen. Partial: only to stimuli close to the trained ones.
I5Scale, outside the ladder. Neurons in the integrating center: cortex in mammals, pallium in birds, whole brain in fish and insects, central brain with optic lobes in cephalopods. Yes: 100 million or more. Partial: 100,000 to under 100 million. No: under 100,000. The centers differ by clade, so the counts are not one quantity; the item only asks whether the center clears two coarse thresholds, and it is worth at most 0.2.

Record integration speed if it is known, but do not score it; speed tracks body size and temperature more than capacity. A large-brained animal tested only on simple tasks scores at most 0.6 on I: size adds 0.2 and cannot carry the term.

Unity (U)

U1Funneling hierarchy, the entry rung. Sensory streams converge on a central hub through mapped levels. A mapped level is a layer or nucleus in which neighboring receptors, or neighboring points in a sensory field, project to neighboring cells, and which feeds a further such layer; the insect retina, lamina and medulla are three. Yes: three or more. Partial: two. No: a nerve net, or sensory neurons feeding interneurons directly. A No makes U, and A, zero.
U2Whole-system states. States that switch the whole system between modes, with recovery after deprivation; in animals, sleep with recovery sleep. Partial: a whole-system rest state without recovery, or only local quiescence.
U3One agent. The whole body acts toward one goal at a time. Partial: parts keep substantial local control, as octopus arms do.
U4Competing drives. Settles conflicts by the stakes, accepting more risk when hungrier. Partial: a fixed order of priority.
U5Selective attention. Picks one target among distractors and holds it. Partial: orients to stimuli but is captured by each new one.

Stretch items

Yes or no, with no points. Many animals score 1 on a term, so these separate the top of the scale. All three, with A at 0.95 or above, give Very high. An untested stretch item is not counted, and the result notes that Very high would follow if it were shown.

S+Image-forming sense. Resolves the features of an object at a distance, as a camera-type eye or echolocation does.
I+Analogical reasoning. Matches relations between relations without being trained on that task.
U+Uncertainty monitoring. Declines, delays or seeks more information on hard trials more often than on easy ones.

Constructs

Three extra rules. A receptor means a physical transducer the system carries, such as a camera, microphone or motion sensor; input handed to it already digitized counts at most Partial on S1, and channels with no transducer count at most Partial on S2. Learning counts only if it happens inside the system during its own operation, so retraining done elsewhere and installed by update does not count. I5 does not apply, because an artificial unit is not a neuron; Integration is the sum of I1 to I4 divided by 0.8, so a construct can still reach 1. Construct scores on the behavioral items are upper bounds, since a system trained to produce a behavior can show it without the property behind it (Butlin et al. 2026).

Steps 2 to 4: The three self tracks

Capacity is one axis. The self is three, each scored on its own and held below what the capacity band supports.

  • Bodily-agentive (B): being a distinct body and the author of one's own actions, up to recognizing oneself. Gallagher's minimal self, Damasio's core self.
  • Interpersonal (IP): representing other agents as agents, an "I" set against a "you," up to modeling what others perceive. Neisser's interpersonal self.
  • Narrative-conceptual (NC): the self extended in time and built in a social, linguistic frame. Gallagher's narrative self, Damasio's autobiographical self, Neisser's conceptual self.

Evidence rules. These steps score rungs, not points. A track reaches a rung when an indicator for that rung is shown and the rung below is reached.

  • An untested indicator does not raise a rung.
  • A result from a single study or a single lab, or a test that left out a component, gives a range down to the rung below, at low confidence. This replication rule applies to every indicator. For self-recognition, a failed close replication counts as a Moderate indicator, which is where the magpie lands.
  • A contested reading, one that could be explained by reading the other's behavior alone, counts toward the upper end of the range only.
  • Evidence from a related species (R) is recorded but does not set a rung.

These rules are stricter than Step 1's, where R-tagged and single-study evidence may set an item at lowest confidence. The difference is deliberate. A capacity item is one of five and moves a term by at most 0.2, while a single self indicator can set a whole rung, and the rungs are where the contested claims sit.

For scent self-recognition, the modified own odor must draw more interest than the added odor alone, or novelty has not been ruled out.

Three rules that keep the tracks honest

  1. The substrate cap. No track is scored above what the capacity band supports. Apply it after all three tracks are scored, and record the raw and capped rungs side by side. The principle is Damasio's nesting: no autobiographical self without a core self.

  2. The ant rule. The interpersonal track scores self-other representation, never social activity. An ant colony's constant pheromone signaling is coordination with no "I versus you" model, so an individual ant stays Low on IP despite maximal social behavior.
  3. The symbolic cap. A narrative self is built in symbols, so NC cannot reach High unless language (N4) or naming is shown. Memory and planning carry NC to Moderate on their own. Naming, vocal labels for specific individuals as in dolphin signature whistles and African elephant name-like calls, lifts the cap but does not itself score High.

Self states

The profile is named by checking these in order and taking the first that fits. When a track is a range that crosses High, name the state at each end.

  • Integrated self: all three tracks High, with SIU High or above. Whether the tracks fuse in behavior is not scored; no indicator for that exists. The name avoids "personhood," which carries moral and legal weight the instrument does not measure.
  • Narrative self: NC High.
  • Social self: IP High.
  • Identified self: B High, self-recognition in any modality.
  • Implicit/situated self: B at least Low, no track at High. Most animals land here.
  • None: the gate failed, or B is None.

The wrasse is why three tracks beat one. Its reported mark-test pass may be real bodily self-recognition, and it shows no narrative self, so a single score has to call it either self-aware or not, and either answer is wrong. On three tracks the pass counts on the bodily track, at a range because every result comes from one lab, and the argument about whether a small-brained fish is "self-aware" mostly dissolves. The ant is why the interpersonal rule matters: score social activity and the ant breaks the model; score representation and it lands at Low.

Validation plan

Reliability is necessary and is not the same as validity. Before the bands are used as results, the worksheet needs four checks:

  1. Agreement. Independent surveyors score the same species from the same evidence, and agreement on bands is measured with Krippendorff's alpha.
  2. Hold-out cases. The reference cases each shaped a design decision, so they are a development set. The test set has to be species chosen and scored after the cutoffs were frozen.
  3. Convergence. Bands and self profiles compared with the multidimensional profiles of Birch, Schnell and Clayton (2020), Veit and colleagues (2025) for corvids, and Dung and Newen (2023). Different dimensions, but the gross ordering should agree.
  4. The ladders. Whether the Integration and Unity items really run in order of difficulty is testable with Mokken scale analysis, once the species database has been rescored item by item on this worksheet.

Open questions

  • Is unity a threshold or a slope? The floor is set by rule. If unity in nature fades continuously, the entry rung is a defensible convention rather than a natural boundary.
  • Superorganisms. Whether a eusocial colony should ever be scored as a unit is unresolved. The current rule scores the individual.
  • Thin narrative evidence. Evidence for a temporally extended self is thin outside about a dozen well-studied species, so expect many low-confidence NC scores.
  • Splitting the narrative track. Patient K.C. could not recall a single experienced event yet rated his own traits accurately (Tulving 1993), and scrub jays show episodic-like memory with no sign of a self-concept. NC merges a temporal self and a conceptual self that come apart in both directions.
  • Hierarchy. Laurenzi, Raffone, Gallagher and Chiarella (2025) argue the dimensions of the animal self are not hierarchically ordered. The substrate cap disagrees at one point, and a species with a High narrative self on a Low capacity would show the cap to be wrong.
  • Affect. SIU has no evaluative dimension. Whether a system's states feel good or bad is outside its scope.
